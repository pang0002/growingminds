Our approach

Why a group of three

Small enough to be personal

Three children to one of us. Your child gets close attention, and the plan is built around them individually — not around what the group is doing.

Real enough to practise on

A child working on communication needs somebody to communicate with. Joining in, waiting a turn, coping when another child takes the toy — none of that can be practised one to one.

Areas of focus

What we work on

Communication

Understanding, expressing, asking for what you need, and being understood by someone who isn't family.

Play and joining in

Playing alongside, then playing with. Sharing attention, taking a turn, following another child's idea.

Attention and early learning

Staying with an activity, following an instruction, and the early thinking skills a classroom builds on.

Emotions and behaviour

Coping with a change of plan, managing frustration, and what's driving the behaviour rather than just the behaviour.

Classroom skills

Sitting, listening, waiting, lining up, tidying away, and moving between activities without falling apart.

Independence

Bag, shoes, snack, toilet — the daily things that decide whether a placement works.

FAQ

Common questions

Do we need a diagnosis to start?

No. Many of the children here don't have one. We don't diagnose either — in Malaysia that sits with paediatricians and doctors, and if we think you should see one, we'll say so and help you get there.

Can my child attend kindergarten as well?

Yes — most of the children in this programme are in school during the morning, and come to us in the afternoon to build the skills that morning is asking of them. We work directly with your child's school, so the two aren't running separately: what we're building here is meant to show up in the classroom, not stay in our room. Some children are still working towards that transition and aren't in a school placement yet — we support that step by step, at their pace.

What approaches do you use?

A child-led approach, so your child is an active partner rather than a passive recipient — combined with the structure of ABA principles and techniques, and a strengths-based lens throughout. In practice, that means we first build the skills your child is missing — the actual blockage to learning — and use ABA-informed strategies specifically for behaviour that's getting in the way. We start with what your child can already do, not just what they can't.

How will I know it's working?

Goals are agreed with you in writing at the start, and we formally review progress against them every three months. You'll always know where things stand — and if something isn't working, we'll say so and change it rather than waiting for the next review.

How long does a child usually stay?

It genuinely depends on the child — their starting point, their progress, and how closely we, you and the school are able to work together. Some children move through more quickly, some need longer, and we won't put a number on it just to give you a tidy answer. What we can promise is that we're reviewing this with you every three months, not leaving you to wonder.

Who will be with my child?

A trained interventionist, working under the supervision of a lead interventionist and an educational psychologist. Your child has a consistent person they see each session, and that person's work is overseen at two levels above them — it isn't left to one individual's judgement alone.

Not sure if this is the right fit?

Tell us your child's age, what you're noticing, and what you'd like help with. We'll tell you honestly which programme fits — including if the answer is none of them.
