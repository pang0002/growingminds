The uncomfortable questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you diagnose ADHD or autism?

No. That sits with paediatricians and doctors. We map how your child learns and turn it into a plan for home and school. If we think you need a diagnostic assessment, we'll say so and point you to the right person.

Do I need a diagnosis first?

No. Many of the children we see don't have one and never need one.

My child already has a diagnosis. Is this still useful?

Often it's the most useful thing families do next. A diagnosis explains a pattern. It rarely tells you what to change about Tuesday evening. That's our work.

Do we need an assessment before joining a club?

No. Clubs are open to any child in the age range. If we notice something in the group you'd want to know about, we'll tell you privately — but nobody is assessed without your consent, and we don't upsell from the club floor.

Do you do IQ testing?

We use standardised cognitive assessments, yes — usually as part of a Learning Profile. What we don't do is hand you a number and leave. The number matters much less than the pattern underneath it, and that's what the report explains. If you need a cognitive assessment on its own for a school or an application, we offer that separately.

Is this tutoring?

No. A tutor teaches content. We work on the skills underneath it — attention, memory, planning, regulation, confidence. Some children need both, and we'll say so.

Is this therapy?

No. We're not a mental health service. If your child needs one, we'll help you find it.

Will my child know they're being assessed?

They'll know they're visiting someone who helps children with learning, and we'll help you word it. In the room it's puzzles, games, drawing and talking. Most children enjoy it. Some ask to come back.

What if you find something serious?

We'll tell you clearly, in person, without softening it into meaninglessness — and we won't leave you to work out the next step alone.
