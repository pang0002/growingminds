Ready to Learn Club

Start it. Stick with it. Finish it.

You've heard it from the teacher and you've seen it at the kitchen table. He's bright. He's just not getting it done.

That's almost never about trying harder. It's about a set of skills — planning, holding instructions, noticing you've drifted, coming back — that school assumes children already have and mostly doesn't teach. We teach them. With Lego, cardboard, cooking and a stopwatch, because that's how they stick.

Come if your child

Is bright, and can't get started · forgets the instruction about thirty seconds after you give it · loses things constantly · gives up the moment it gets hard · starts everything and finishes very little.

Eight weeks, eight things to practise

Holding two steps, then three. Making a plan before touching anything. Starting without being told. Staying with something genuinely boring. Catching your own mistake. Knowing where your stuff is. Working out how long something will take. Finishing.

What it looks like on a Wednesday

A build with a written brief and a deadline. A recipe with steps deliberately out of order. A task where we hand out the instructions once, out loud, and don't repeat them. A challenge designed to go wrong in week five so everyone gets to practise starting again.

What you might notice at home

Homework that starts without a negotiation.
