What a psychoeducational assessment actually tells you

By Growing Minds Team 3 September 2026 6 min read

A psychoeducational assessment can sound intimidating from the outside — lots of subtests, unfamiliar scores, and a thick report at the end. Here's what's actually happening at each stage.

Before the session

We start with a conversation with parents about what's prompting the assessment: a teacher's observation, a struggle at home, or just wanting a clearer picture of how your child learns.

During the assessment

Your child works through a series of tasks — some verbal, some visual, some timed — designed to map out strengths and areas that need more support. It's paced to feel more like a series of games than a test.

Reading the report

The report translates all of that into a plain-language summary: what your child's profile looks like, what it means day-to-day, and concrete recommendations for home and school.

If anything in the report is unclear, that's exactly what the follow-up conversation with our team is for — you should leave with a clear next step, not just a stack of numbers.
