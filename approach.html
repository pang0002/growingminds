Building the skills behind learning, confidence and independence

Children get sorted early. Bright or slow, easy or difficult, trying hard or not trying at all. The labels arrive long before anyone has actually looked at how the child in front of them learns.

We think that's backwards. We also think it's expensive — in confidence, in wasted years, in children who decide by the age of eight that they're the stupid one.

So we start by understanding. Properly, with the tools of educational psychology, and with the time it takes. Only then do we suggest what to do.

The Research Base

What Our Work Is Built On

We didn't invent these constructs. We translate them — into skills a parent can recognise, and into work a child enjoys doing.

Cognitive Foundations

Working memory, processing speed, attention and reasoning. This is why a bright child can still be drowning: reasoning can be strong while working memory isn't, and school reads the result as carelessness.

Executive Function

The skills that let a child hold an instruction, resist a distraction, plan ahead and change approach when the first one fails.

Self-Regulation & Metacognition

Planning before starting, noticing while working, checking afterwards. Among the highest-impact, lowest-cost things a child can learn.

Social-Emotional Competence

Self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and relationship skills.

Language & Communication

Chronically under-recognised. A child who "gets angry instead of explaining" is often a child with a language gap nobody has named.
